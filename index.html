Mapping Pop Art

Sarah Pasha

This map is inspired by Andy Warhol's Pop Art style. It uses bright colors, bold outlines, and simple shapes to make familiar places look different. This project looks at four locations connected to Warhol and shows how the map changes across different places and zoom levels.

Inspiration

My map was inspired by Andy Warhol's Marilyn Diptych, Banana, and Camouflage series. Marilyn Diptych inspired my bright pink and cyan colors. Banana influenced my yellow water and black road outlines. The Camouflage series inspired the different shades of green used across the land. Together, these artworks helped me create a colorful Pop Art basemap.

Pittsburgh is Andy Warhol's hometown and the location of the Andy Warhol Museum. The bright roads and yellow rivers help show the city's layout.

Montauk, New York, was another place connected to Warhol. The pink land areas and yellow water stand out along the coastline.

New York City shows how the Pop Art style works in a busy urban area. The bright roads create a strong pattern across the city.

Big Sur, California, was also important to Warhol. Here, the different shades of green help show the natural landscape along the coast.
